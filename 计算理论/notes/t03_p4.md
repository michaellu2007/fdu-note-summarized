### 第 69 页

**Example:** Show that $L = \{a^n b^n c^n : n \ge 0\}$ is not CFL.

**Proof:** Suppose that $L = L(G)$ for some CFG $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$.

（状态/结构图描述）一条横向的长条被分为三个相邻的方框：左框内为 $a$ —— $a$，中框内为 $b$ —— $b$，右框内为 $c$ —— $c$。长条上方有一条贯穿左框左端到右框右端的双向箭头，标注 "length $> n$"。

| 方框 | 内容 | 说明 |
|---|---|---|
| 左框 | $a$ —— $a$ | 对应 $a$ 段 |
| 中框 | $b$ —— $b$ | 对应 $b$ 段 |
| 右框 | $c$ —— $c$ | 对应 $c$ 段 |

长条上方标注：length $> n$。

Let $n > \dfrac{\phi(G)^{|V-\Sigma|}}{3}$.
Then $w = a^n b^n c^n \in L(G)$ and can has a representation $w = uvxyz$ such that
- $vy \ne e$
- $uv^n x y^n z \in L(G)$ for each $n = 0, 1, 2, \cdots$

### 第 70 页

**Question: what about $v, y$?**

Case 1. Contain all three kinds of symbols
Case 2. Contain 2 kinds of symbols

- $v, y$ contains occurrences of all three symbols $a, b, c$.
  $\Rightarrow$ at least one of $v, y$ must contain at least two of them.
  $\Rightarrow$ order error in $uv^2 x y^2 z$.
- $v, y$ contains occurrences of some but not all of them
  $\Rightarrow$ $uv^2 x y^2 z$ has unequal number of $a$'s, $b$'s and $c$'s.

**Contradiction!!**

### 第 71 页

**Remark:**
**Proving that a language is not CFL**
- Let $L$ be the proposed CFL
- There is some $n$, by the pumping lemma
- Choose a string $s$, longer than $n$ symbols, in the language $L$
- Using the pumping lemma, construct a new string $s$ that is not in the language

### 第 72 页

**Example:** Show $L = \{a^n \mid n \text{ is prime }\}$ is not Context-free.

**Proof:** Take a prime $p > \phi(G)^{|V-\Sigma|}$, where $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ is CFG and $L = L(G)$.
Then $w = a^p$ can be written as prescribed by Pumping theorem, $w = uvxyz$ and $vy \ne e$.
Suppose that $vy = a^q$ and $uxz = a^r$ where $p$ and $q$ are natural numbers and $q > 0$.
Then the theorem states that $r + nq$ is prime, for all $n \ge 0$.

**Contradiction!!**

**Remark:**
Any CFL over a single-letter alphabet is regular.

**Theorem:** The CFL are not closed under intersection or complementation.

**Proof:** 1) **Intersection**

**Counterexample**
$$L_1 = \{a^n b^n c^m : m, n \ge 0\}$$
$$= \{a^n b^n : n \ge 0\} \circ c^*$$
$$L_2 = \{a^m b^n c^n : m, n \ge 0\}$$

（旁注框）

### 第 77 页

**Question:**
Given CFG $G$ and string $w$ to determine if $w \in L(G)$

- Note that constructing a PDA from $G$ does not in general solve this problem since the PDA will generally be *nondeterministic*.
- *Brute-Force Method:*
  Check all parse trees of height up to some upper limit depending on $G$ and $|w|$ ( Exponentially costly)
- *The dynamic programming algorithm:*
  1. Transform G into Chomsky normal form (CNF)
  2. Apply a special algorithm for CNF grammars

### 第 78 页

**Definition:** A grammar is in **Chomsky normal form**(CNF) iff
$$R \subseteq (V - \Sigma) \times V^2.$$

**Remark:**
- The right-hand side of a rule in a CFG in Chomsky normal form must have length two.
- No grammar in CNF would be able to produce strings of length less than 2 and CFL containing such strings cannot be generated by grammar in CNF.

### 第 79 页

**Theorem:** For any CFG $G$ there is a CFG $G'$ in CNF such that $L(G') = L(G) - (\Sigma \cup \{e\})$. Furthermore, the construction of $G'$ can be carried out in time polynomial in size of $G$.

**Proof:**
We shall show how to transform any given CFG $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ into CNF.

We eliminate the following in order:
1. *Long rules*: of the form $A \to \alpha$ where $|\alpha| > 2$
2. *e-rules*: of the form $A \to e$
3. *Short rules*: of the form $A \to a$ or $A \to B$

### 第 80 页

$\square$ **Transforming a CFG into CNF**

**Step 1:** Chop up all long rules

Let $A \to B_1 B_2 \cdots B_n \in R$, where $B_1, \cdots B_n \in V$ and $n \ge 3$.
Replace this rules with $n - 1$ new rules, namely:
$$A \to B_1 A_1,$$
$$A_1 \to B_2 A_2,$$
$$\cdots$$
$$A_{n-2} \to B_{n-1} B_{n-2}.$$
where $A_1, \cdots , A_n$ are newly introduced nonterminals.

### 第 81 页

**Step 2:** To eliminate $e$-rules

a. Determine *the set of erasable nonterminals*
$$\mathcal{E} = \{A \in V - \Sigma : A \Rightarrow^* e\}$$
— is the set of all nonterminals that may drive the empty string.

> **Algorithm:** Computing the set of erasable nonterminals
>
> $\mathcal{E} := \emptyset$
> **While** there is a rule $A \Rightarrow \alpha$ with $\alpha \in \mathcal{E}^*$ and $A \notin \mathcal{E}$ **do**
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;add $A$ to $\mathcal{E}$

### 第 82 页

b. For each rule of form $A \to BC$ or $A \to CB$ with $B \in \mathcal{E}$ and $C \in V$, we add the rule $A \to C$.

e.g.
$$A \to BC \quad \Rightarrow \quad A \to BC$$
$$B \in \mathcal{E} \text{ and } C \in V. \qquad\quad A \to C$$

c. Eliminate all $e$-rules.

**Remark:**
Any derivation in the oringinal grammar can be simulated in the new, and vice versa — with one exception: $e$ can not be derived in the language any longer. (Since we may have omitted the $S \to e$ in this process.)

### 第 83 页

**Step 3:** To get rid of the short rules

For each $A \in V$ we compute by a simple closure algorithm, *the set $\mathcal{D}(A)$ of symbols that can de derived from $A$ in grammar*, $\mathcal{D}(A) = \{B \in V : A \Rightarrow^* B\}$

> **Algorithm:** Computing the set $\mathcal{D}(A)$
>
> $\mathcal{D}(A) := \{A\}$
> **While** there is a rule $B \to C$ with $B \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ and $C \notin \mathcal{D}(A)$ **do**
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;add $C$ to $\mathcal{D}(A)$

### 第 84 页

**Remark:**
- For all symbols $A$, $A \in \mathcal{D}(A)$;
- If $a$ be a terminal, then $\mathcal{D}(a) = \{a\}$.

a. If there is a rule $A \to BC$, we replace it weith all possible rules of form:
$$A \to B'C' \text{ with } B' \in \mathcal{D}(B), C' \in \mathcal{D}(C)$$

b. Add rules $S \to BC$ for each rule $A \to BC$ such that $A \in \mathcal{D}(S) - \{S\}$

c. Then drop all short rules $X \to Y$.

**Remark:** the resulting grammar is equivalent to the one before the omission of the short rules with only one exception: we may remove $S \to a$ .

### 第 87 页

$\square$ **Determining $x \in L(G)$ for $G$ in CNF**

- Let $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ be a CFG in CNF.
- Let $x = x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$, $x_i \in \Sigma$ and $n \ge 2$.
- For each $i$ and $s$ such that $1 \le i \le i + s \le n$, define:
$$N[i, i+s] = \{X : X \in V, X \Rightarrow^* x_i \cdots x_{i+s}\}.$$
  - $x \in L(G)$ iff $S \in N[1, n]$.
  - Calculate $N[i, i+s]$ by induction on $s$:
    $$A \in N[i, i+s] \text{ iff } \exists \text{ rule } A \to BC$$
    $$\text{such that } B \in N[i, k]$$
    $$C \in N[k+1, i+s].$$

### 第 88 页

> **Algorithm:** Determine $x \in L(G)$
>
> **For** $i := 1$ **to** $n$ **do** $N[i, i] := \{x_i\}$; all other $N[i, j]$ are initially empty
>
> **For** $s := 1$ **to** $n - 1$ **do**
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**For** $i := 1$ **to** $n - s$ **do**
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**For** $k := i$ **to** $i + s - 1$ **do**
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*If there is a rule $A \to BC \in R$ with $B \in N[i, k]$ and $C \in N[k+1, i+s]$*
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*then add $A$ to $N[i, i+s]$*
>
> Accept $x$ if $S \in N[1, n]$.

—— **Dynamic programming**
