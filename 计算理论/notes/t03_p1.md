### 第 5 页

**Definition:** A context-free grammar(CFG) is a quadruple $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$, where

- $V$ is an alphabet;
- $\Sigma \subseteq V$ is the set of terminal symbols;（slide 中 "terminal" 带下划线）
- $S \in V - \Sigma$ is the start symbol;（slide 中 "start" 带下划线）and
- $R$ is the set of rules,（slide 中 "rules" 带下划线）a finite subset of $(V - \Sigma) \times V^*$.

**Remark:**
- The member of $V - \Sigma$ are called **nonterminals**.
  For any $A \in V - \Sigma$ and $u \in V^*$,
  $$A \to_G u \iff (A, u) \in R.$$

---

### 第 6 页

- For any strings $u, v \in V^*$,
  $$u \Rightarrow_G v \iff \exists x, y \in V^*,\ \text{and } A \in V - \Sigma,\ \text{such that } u = xAy,\ v = xv'y,\ \text{and } A \to_G v'.$$
- $\Rightarrow_G^*$ is the reflexive, transitive closure of $\Rightarrow_G$.
- $w_0 \Rightarrow_G w_1 \Rightarrow_G \cdots \Rightarrow_G w_n$
  — a **derivation** in $G$ of $w_n$ from $w_0$. $n$ be the length of the derivation.
- The **language generated by** $G$,
  $$L(G) = \{w \in \Sigma^* : S \Rightarrow_G^* w\}.$$

$L$ is a **context-free language(CFL)** $\iff \exists$ a context-free grammar(CFG) $G$, such that $L = L(G)$.

---

### 第 7 页

**Example:** Consider the CFG $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ where
$V = \{S, a, b\},\ \Sigma = \{a, b\},\ R = \{S \to aSb,\ S \to e\}$.

$$L(G) = \{a^n b^n : n \ge 0\}$$

（红框标注：$L(G)$ is context-free but not regular）

**Example:** Let $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ where
$V = \{S, (, )\},\ \Sigma = \{(, )\},\ R = \{S \to e,\ S \to SS,\ S \to (S)\}$.

$L(G)$ is the language containing all strings of balanced parentheses.

### 第 8 页

**Example:** Let $G = (\{S, a, b\}, \{a, b\}, R, S)$, where $R = \{S \to e,\ S \to SS,\ S \to aSb,\ S \to bSa\}$.
$$L(G) = \{w \in \{a, b\}^* : w \text{ has the same number of } a\text{'s and } b\text{'s}\}$$

**Proof:**

（红框）
$$w \in L(G) \Rightarrow w \text{ has the same number of } a\text{'s and } b\text{'s.}$$

By Induction on length $k$ of derivation.

(a) $k = 1$

The derivation is $S \Rightarrow e$

$\Rightarrow w = e$ has the same number of $a$'s and $b$'s. ✓

---

### 第 9 页

(b) $k > 1$

Then either:
$$S \Rightarrow SS \Rightarrow^* xy = w$$
$$S \Rightarrow aSb \Rightarrow^* axb = w$$
$$S \Rightarrow bSa \Rightarrow^* bxa = w$$

Since $S \Rightarrow^* x,\ S \Rightarrow^* y$ by derivations of length $< k$

$x, y$ have equal number of $a$'s and $b$'s (IH)

so do $xy$, $axb$, and $bxa$. ✓

---

### 第 10 页

（红框）
$$w \text{ has the same number of } a\text{'s and } b\text{'s} \Rightarrow w \in L(G)$$

By induction on $|w|$.

(a) $|w| = 0$
$$w = e \in L(G)$$
（蓝框：$S \to e$ is a rule.）

(b) $|w| = k + 2$: ($|w|$ must be even)

4 subcase depending on first and last symbols of $w$:

**Case 1** $w = axb$ for some $x \in \Sigma^*$
$$|x| = k,\quad x \text{ has the same number of } a\text{'s and } b\text{'s.} \qquad \Rightarrow\ \text{（红框：} S \Rightarrow^* x\text{）}$$
$$S \Rightarrow aSb \Rightarrow^* axb = w$$

---

### 第 11 页

**Case 2** $w = bxa$ for some $x \in \Sigma^*$ - *similar*

**Case 3** $w = axa$ for some $x \in \Sigma^*$
$$|x| = k,\quad x \text{ has 2 more } b\text{'s than } a\text{'s.}$$
$$x = uv \text{ for some } u \text{ and } v \text{ such that}$$
- $u$ has one more $b$ than $a$.
- $v$ has one more $b$ than $a$.

$$S \Rightarrow^* au \text{ and } S \Rightarrow^* va$$
$$\text{So } S \Rightarrow^* SS \Rightarrow^* auva = w.$$

**Case 4** $w = bxb$ for some $x \in \Sigma^*$ - *similar*

### 第 17 页

**3.2 Parse Tree**

**Example:** If $G$ is the CFG that generates language of balanced parentheses.

Then string $(())()$ can be derived from $S$ by at least two distinct derivations, namely,

（红框内的规则）
$$S \to e,\quad S \to SS,\quad S \to (S)$$
（气泡标注：**Same!**）

$$S \Rightarrow \underline{S}S \Rightarrow (\underline{S})S \Rightarrow ((\underline{S}))S \Rightarrow (()) \underline{S} \Rightarrow (())(\underline{S}) \Rightarrow (())()$$

$$S \Rightarrow S\underline{S} \Rightarrow \underline{S}(S) \Rightarrow (\underline{S})(S) \Rightarrow (\underline{S})() \Rightarrow ((\underline{S}))() \Rightarrow (())()$$

（下划线表示该步被展开的非终结符。）

---

### 第 18 页

Both derivations can be pictured as following:

**语法树（缩进结构描述）**

```
S
├── S
│   ├── (
│   ├── S
│   │   ├── (
│   │   ├── S
│   │   │   └── e
│   │   └── )
│   └── )
└── S
    ├── (
    ├── S
    │   └── e
    └── )
```

- Node: has a label in $V$
- Root: Start symbol
- Leaves: labeled by terminals
- The string
  — *by concatenating the labels of leaves from left to right is called the yield of the parse tree.*
  — **Parse tree**

### 第 19 页

□ **Parse tree:** formal definition

For an arbitrary CFG $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$, we define its parse tree as following:

- This is the parse tree for each $a \in \Sigma$. The single node is both the root and a leaf. The yield of this parse tree is $a$.

  （图示：单个节点 $a$）

- If $A \to e$ is a rule in $R$, then is a parse tree.

  （图示：根 $A$，叶子 $e$，二者由一条边相连）
  - root: labeled $A$
  - leaf: labeled $e$
  - yield: $e$

---

- If

  （图示：$n$ 棵语法树，根分别为 $A_1, A_2, \cdots, A_n$，对应产出为 $y_1, y_2, \cdots, y_n$）

  are parse trees, where $n \ge 1$, with roots labeled $A_1, \cdots, A_n$, respectively, and yields $y_1, \cdots, y_n$, and $A \to A_1 \cdots A_n$ is a rule in $R$, then

  （图示：新建根 $A$，其子节点依次为 $A_1, A_2, \cdots, A_n$，每个子树为 $T_1, \cdots, T_n$）
  - root: labeled $A$
  - yield: $y_1 y_2 \cdots y_n$

### 第 22 页

□ **Derivations D and D' are similar:** formal definition

Let $G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)$ be a CFG,

$$D = x_1 \Rightarrow x_2 \Rightarrow \cdots \Rightarrow x_n$$
$$D' = x_1' \Rightarrow x_2' \Rightarrow \cdots \Rightarrow x_n'$$

be two derivations, where $x_i, x_i' \in V^*$ for $i = 1 \cdots n$, $x_1, x_1' \in V - \Sigma$, and $x_n, x_n' \in \Sigma^*$.

1) $D$ **precedes** $D'$ ($D \prec D'$) $\iff \exists\, 1 < k < n$, such that
   - for all $i \ne k$, we have $x_i = x_i'$.
   - $x_{k-1} = x_{k-1}' = uAvBw$, where $u, v, w \in V^*$, and $A, B \in V - \Sigma$.

---

### 第 23 页

- $x_k = uyvBw$, where $A \to y \in R$.
- $x_k' = uAvzw$, where $B \to z \in R$.
- $x_{k+1} = x_{k+1}' = uyvzw$.

2) $D$ and $D'$ are **similar** $\iff (D, D')$, belong in the reflexive, symmetric, transitive closure of $\prec$.

**Remark:**
- Similarity is an equivalence relation.
- Derivations in the same equivalence class under similarity have the same parse tree.
